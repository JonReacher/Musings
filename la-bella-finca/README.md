# La Bella Finca

A cigar business simulator, from one small tobacco field to a global brand. Open `index.html` in any browser. There is nothing to install.

## What it does

The scenes, people and street views are painted in a stylized look: soft light, warm shadows, cyan skies and tropical color.

- **Grow tobacco.** Buy land in a valley four times the size of the old one: river bottoms, eastern hills, a lake, old fields and worn barns for sale, and Don Celestino's farm next door. Plow, seed and tend the plants through real seasons, weather and pests. Soil, drainage and crop rotation all matter.
- **Cure, ferment and age.** Harvest in three primings, each with its own leaf quality, manage the curing barn's humidity, turn the fermentation piles and age the leaf.
- **Hire skilled hands.** Operations → People offers a skilled and a highly skilled hire for every piece of farm work that isn't a business decision: field crews, barn tenders, a sprayer for blue mold, a pilón turner and an aging-house hand who sorts and bales finished leaf. Skill jobs open only once you have done them yourself, so you learn the work before you hand it off. What to plant, ferment and sell stays with you, until you hire a farm manager.
- **Run the workshop from one place.** Operations → Workshop shows the rolling queue (what is being rolled now, what is next, when each order finishes and goes on sale), an order form (pick a blend, a shape drawn to scale with its leaf, speed and seconds, and a quantity), the cedar room (a resting timeline and when each batch goes on sale) and keep-on-hand targets.
- **Hire a farm manager.** Once the whole chain stands (curing barn, fermentation house, aging warehouse, rolling workshop and roadside stand), a manager can run the leaf through it by your standing orders: which grades go into the pilón, which are baled, which bales are kept for your own cigars, when lesser bales are sold or kept for cheaper blends, how much of each leaf to keep in reserve, when to sell for cash and whether to clear the barns at harvest. A skilled manager misreads leaf now and then and lets work wait; a highly skilled one reads leaf at a glance and gets better prices. Leaf in your blends, and leaf you bought, is never sold.
- **Learn from Don Aurelio.** Your neighbor teaches lessons, side missions and mini-games. You start poor, and getting good takes his help.
- **Roll cigars.** Blend wrapper, binder and filler at the blending table, hire rollers of different skill, and rest the cigars in the cedar room.
- **Run the business.** A workshop dashboard follows leaf to rolling to stock to sales and flags the bottleneck. A roadside stand has real weekdays, prices for first quality and seconds, named regulars, and offers you can weigh against your shelf prices.
- **Build a brand.** Piece together a logo, name, palette and band in the brand studio.
- **Open a shop in town.** Old Señor Ibarra's hardware store on Calle Real in San Rafael is for sale for $9,500. Buy it, fit it out and run it as a small first shop: cheap to learn retail on, before the city. Its sales count toward the city's goals.

- **Keep the books.** Operations → Finance grows with the company. On the farm it is a cash book: what came in, what went out, and how long the cash lasts. Its History view keeps every month since the books began (up to six years): money in and out by year, by month and by category. Once you sell under your own name it adds a monthly profit and loss, what each cigar earns after leaf and rolling, how busy the workshop is, break-even and a 13-week cash forecast.

## The late game: Puerto Celeste

- **Earn the city.** Operations → Growth lists what the capital will ask of you: a brand, $25,000 of cigar sales in a year, a reputation of 55, 26 full Monday deliveries in a row, a cigar rated 4 stars by 100 customers, $30,000 in cash with no bank debt, and Don Aurelio's trust. When you have all seven, he writes a letter, and the city opens on the map itself: two more sections of land south of the farms, down the coast road.
- **Find a storefront.** Walk the city on the map and click the buildings. Twelve flagship storefronts are for rent or sale across five districts: the Old Town, the Malecón, the financial district, the market barrio and the hills. They differ in size, condition, rent, foot traffic and customers. Brokers exaggerate, so count the traffic yourself, hire an inspector and talk to the neighbors before you sign. Leases are negotiated: free months for the fit-out, a longer term for less rent, a bad clause struck out.
- **Fit out your flagship.** Paint the front in the color you want people to remember (the Old Town allows only its heritage palette), pick the shutters and awnings, and choose the style of the room, the humidor, a lounge, a bar, a roller in the window, a story wall, the shop front, the sign, a till system and backup power, with a live view of the shop as you plan it.
- **Run it like a business.** Hire a manager and tobacconists, set par levels for the weekly truck from the farm, price for the city, buy marketing, hold events. The first of each month brings rent, bills, tax and a statement.
- **Learn from Doña Victoria Arriaga.** Your mentor in business teaches margins, leases, break-even, cash, positioning, people, marketing, distribution, scale and export, with case quizzes built from fresh numbers and a monthly review of your own books.
- **Be known how you choose.** Pick what the brand stands for, from the farmer's cigar to a luxury house, and keep the price, street, room and cigars telling one story. Then grow: city accounts that pay on terms, a magazine rating, the international tobacco fair, and leaf from other growers.
- **Run a company.** In the city the books become a company's: an income statement, a balance sheet and a cash-flow statement that tie to the cent, a KPI dashboard (margins, days of stock and receivables, the cash cycle, leverage), a monthly budget with variances, a performance report for each store (sales per m², occupancy cost, conversion, payback), an investment case for every storefront (payback and NPV), and a bank that offers a term loan with a covenant.
- **Go global.** After the tobacco fair a world map opens, the only map in the game. Pick a city abroad (Miami, Toronto, Madrid, Berlin, Dubai or Hong Kong), sign a distributor, or lease a shop of your own there. Shops abroad pay high rents and wages, start unknown and usually lose money for months. Four markets abroad, a rating of 90 and $500,000 of sales in a year make a global name. A group reports by market: results and contribution per country, currencies that move every month (with forward contracts to lock a rate), what a cigar costs landed abroad through a distributor or your own shop, a quarterly board pack, what the company is worth, and investors who will buy a stake for growth money.

## Notes

- Games are saved in this browser. Use "Copy save code" in the Games menu to move a game to another browser.
- Saves from the old, smaller valley still load: your land sits in the northwest corner of the new one.
- One year of game time takes about ten minutes at normal speed.
- The Games menu has test starts for the late game: a farm at the city's door, one with a flagship already open, and one going global.
